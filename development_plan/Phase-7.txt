# Phase 7 — Demand, Inventory and Opportunity Analysis

Responsibility: Member 2 — Primary owner

Support: Sameera (Member 1) — review the analysis, findings, and potential features for the Random Forest model.

## Objective

Analyze the Phase 6 ODE dataset to understand which operational factors are associated with successful PTL-to-FTL conversion. The goal is to identify demand patterns, inventory constraints, truck-utilization trends, and potentially useful predictive features before proceeding to Phase 8.

## Work to be done

### 1. Analyze the ODE dataset

- Load `ode_opportunities.csv` generated in Phase 6.
- Inspect its dimensions, column types, missing values, duplicates, and target distribution.
- Calculate descriptive statistics for numerical features such as remaining weight capacity, remaining volume capacity, inventory availability, proposed cargo weight, and projected utilization.
- Analyze categorical features such as truck class, route, destination, and product category.
- Identify class imbalance in `Became_FTL`.

### 2. Analyze demand patterns

- Study historical demand by SKU, destination, and time.
- Identify products and destinations with consistently high or low demand.
- Examine demand variability and recurring patterns across the 90-day simulation.
- Investigate whether historical demand, demand-to-stock ratios, and destination-level demand are associated with FTL conversion.
- Identify potentially useful demand-related features for Phase 8.

### 3. Analyze inventory availability

- Compare available inventory against eligible open order quantities.
- Identify products for which stock availability frequently limits consolidation.
- Analyze whether sufficient inventory exists to fill the remaining truck capacity.
- Examine how inventory availability, SKU quantities, and product physical attributes relate to successful opportunities.

### 4. Analyze truck utilization

- Examine current and projected utilization by weight and volume.
- Compare utilization across truck classes, routes, and destinations.
- Identify situations where weight capacity is sufficient but volume capacity is restrictive, and vice versa.
- Analyze how remaining capacity and proposed cargo characteristics differ between successful and unsuccessful FTL opportunities.

### 5. Analyze FTL conversion outcomes

Compare records where `Became_FTL = 1` against records where `Became_FTL = 0`.

Investigate differences in:

- Remaining weight and volume capacity.
- Available inventory and eligible open demand.
- Proposed SKU combinations and cargo quantities.
- Truck class and route.
- Destination demand and historical demand patterns.
- Planning time and other relevant operational features.

The objective is to identify patterns that may help predict conversion, not to assume that every observed association is causal.

### 6. Perform statistical analysis

- Calculate distributions, means, medians, quartiles, variance, and standard deviation for relevant numerical variables.
- Examine correlations among numerical features.
- Compare feature distributions across the two target classes.
- Use suitable statistical tests where justified by the data and their assumptions.
- Investigate redundant or highly correlated features that may require attention during Phase 8.

### 7. Visualize the findings

Create relevant plots, including:

- Histograms and box plots for numerical feature distributions.
- Bar charts for truck classes, destinations, and product categories.
- Scatter plots to examine relationships between remaining capacity, inventory, demand, and projected utilization.
- Correlation heatmaps for numerical variables.
- Class-wise comparisons of important features for `Became_FTL`.
- Time-series plots of historical demand and conversion trends where the data supports them.

Each visualization should answer a specific analytical question rather than exist only for presentation.

### 8. Compare candidate SKU combinations

- Represent each candidate's proposed SKU set.
- Use Jaccard similarity to compare SKU combinations across relevant candidates or planning runs.
- Investigate whether similar SKU sets have different outcomes under different capacity, inventory, demand, or route conditions.
- Avoid treating similar SKU sets as independent evidence when they share the same underlying operational event.

### 9. Identify candidate features for Phase 8

Based on the analysis, prepare a shortlist of features that may help the models predict FTL conversion.

Examples include:

- Remaining weight capacity and remaining volume capacity.
- Current and projected utilization.
- Available inventory and eligible open order quantities.
- Proposed cargo weight and volume.
- Demand-to-stock ratios.
- SKU physical characteristics.
- Route compatibility and destination-level demand.
- Historical demand aggregates and variability.
- Relevant planning-time features.

For each suggested feature, document what it represents, why it may be useful, and whether it is available at prediction time.

Important: Do not use future outcomes, post-dispatch information, or the `Became_FTL` target itself to construct predictive features. Phase 7 may inspect outcomes to understand the data, but any feature used for modelling must be available at the actual prediction timestamp.

### 10. Document the conclusions

Summarize:

- The main patterns observed in demand and inventory.
- The most important truck-capacity constraints.
- The differences observed between successful and unsuccessful opportunities.
- Potentially useful predictive features.
- Data-quality issues and limitations.
- Questions to investigate during feature engineering and model training.

## Deliverables

- `notebooks/phase7_opportunity_analysis.ipynb`
- `reports/phase7_opportunity_analysis.md`
- Relevant figures saved under `reports/figures/`
- A documented shortlist of candidate features for Phase 8.

## Expected outcome

At the end of Phase 7, Member 2 should understand the characteristics of the ODE dataset, the operational factors associated with PTL-to-FTL conversion, and which features are worth investigating further.

These findings will guide Phase 8's shared baseline feature engineering, after which Sameera will train Random Forest and Member 2 will train XGBoost using the agreed baseline features.
