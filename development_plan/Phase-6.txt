# Phase 6 — Build the Opportunity Detection Engine (ODE) Dataset

Responsibility: Member 2 — Primary owner

Support: Sameera (Member 1) — review dataset logic, feature definitions, and validation.

## Objective

Transform the synthetic operational data generated in Phase 5 into a consolidated dataset of feasible PTL-to-FTL opportunities. Each row should represent a candidate opportunity consisting of a partially loaded truck and a proposed combination of additional SKUs and quantities.

## Work to be done

### 1. Load and understand the operational data

- Load the relevant CSV files generated in Phase 5.
- Inspect schemas, data types, primary keys, foreign keys, and relationships.
- Identify the tables required for products, inventory, orders, trucks, routes, shipments/loading, planning runs, and historical demand.
- Select the relevant columns and document how the tables relate to one another.

### 2. Integrate the data

- Merge relevant DataFrames using the correct identifiers.
- Associate each opportunity with its truck, route, planning timestamp, inventory state, and eligible orders.
- Prevent duplicate rows and unintended multiplication of quantities during joins.
- Preserve the relationship between each opportunity and its underlying planning run.

### 3. Calculate truck capacity and utilization

For each truck at the relevant planning timestamp, calculate:

- Maximum payload capacity in kilograms.
- Current loaded weight.
- Remaining weight capacity.
- Maximum volume capacity.
- Current loaded volume.
- Remaining volume capacity.
- Weight utilization percentage.
- Volume utilization percentage.

Identify partially loaded trucks with remaining capacity. A truck must be evaluated using both weight and volume constraints.

### 4. Calculate inventory and demand availability

- Determine available inventory for each relevant SKU at the planning timestamp.
- Identify eligible open orders and their remaining quantities.
- Match inventory to demand for compatible destinations and routes.
- Calculate the maximum feasible quantity of each SKU that could be added to a truck.
- Ensure that proposed quantities do not exceed either available inventory or eligible open order quantities.

Use the operational quantities and event history consistently. Do not introduce cancellation logic.

### 5. Generate candidate SKU combinations

This is one of the most important tasks in Phase 6.

Develop a candidate-generation algorithm that proposes additional cargo for partially loaded trucks.

For example, the algorithm might generate:

- Candidate A: SKU-101 × 100 units + SKU-105 × 50 units.
- Candidate B: SKU-103 × 80 units.
- Candidate C: SKU-108 × 40 units + SKU-110 × 30 units.

These are illustrative examples only.

The algorithm should:

1. Find SKUs with available inventory and compatible outstanding demand.
2. Check whether each SKU can be transported on the truck's route.
3. Calculate feasible quantities using unit weight and unit volume.
4. Generate individual-SKU or multi-SKU combinations.
5. Reject combinations that exceed remaining weight or volume capacity.
6. Avoid combinations that allocate the same stock or order quantity inconsistently across competing candidates.
7. Record the proposed SKUs, quantities, and resulting truck utilization for each candidate.

Candidate generation should use a documented rule-based or search-based method. It does not need to use machine learning; the ML models will score the generated candidates in Phase 9.

### 6. Construct the target variable: `Became_FTL`

- Define what constitutes an FTL outcome for a candidate.
- Establish the prediction timestamp and the subsequent observation window.
- Use subsequent operational events to determine whether the candidate's outcome satisfies the FTL definition.
- Assign `Became_FTL = 1` to qualifying positive outcomes and `Became_FTL = 0` to qualifying negative outcomes.
- Document how unresolved or insufficiently observed cases are handled rather than automatically labelling them negative.

The target must reflect the defined operational outcome. Future events may be used to construct the target, but must not be included as input features.

### 7. Create the ODE dataset

Create a consolidated dataset with one row per candidate opportunity, using a unique candidate ID.

Include relevant fields such as:

- Candidate ID and planning-run ID.
- Planning timestamp.
- Truck ID and truck class.
- Route and destination.
- Candidate SKU IDs and proposed quantities.
- Current and remaining truck weight capacity.
- Current and remaining truck volume capacity.
- Relevant inventory and open-demand features.
- Candidate added weight and volume.
- Projected weight and volume utilization.
- Feasibility indicators.
- `Became_FTL` target.

The dataset may also contain additional features needed for Phase 8. Document the schema and the meaning of each field.

### 8. Validate the dataset

Perform checks for:

- Duplicate candidate IDs and unintended duplicate opportunities.
- Missing or invalid references.
- Negative inventory or capacity values.
- Proposed quantities exceeding eligible inventory or order quantities.
- Weight or volume capacity violations.
- Route and destination incompatibilities.
- Incorrect target labels or target leakage.
- Duplicate allocation of inventory across candidates when constructing a single operational recommendation.
- Class balance of `Became_FTL`.
- Whether the target can be determined within the observation window.

### Deliverables

- `data/processed/ode_opportunities.csv`
- Candidate-generation and dataset-construction notebook or scripts.
- A documented feature dictionary and dataset schema.
- Target-definition documentation for `Became_FTL`.
- A Phase 6 validation report.

## Expected outcome

At the end of Phase 6, CargoSense should have a dataset where each row represents a proposed PTL-to-FTL opportunity for a specific truck and additional cargo combination. This dataset becomes the input for Phase 7 analysis, Phase 8 feature engineering, and Phase 9 model training.
